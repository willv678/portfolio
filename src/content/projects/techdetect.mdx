I built TechDetect the summer before senior year of high school, during the STEM@GTRI internship at the Georgia Tech Research Institute. The idea: a camera at the classroom door that takes attendance by itself.

<YouTube id="pS-RZ7sxLiQ" title="TechDetect demo" />

## How it works

- **Enroll:** a script captures 75 photos of your face, converts them to grayscale and shrinks them so they're easier to match.
- **Recognize:** OpenCV watches the camera feed and labels each face it's confident about. Below a set confidence it says *unknown* rather than guessing.
- **Track:** sign-ins and sign-outs go to a MySQL database, and a small Flask site behind a login shows the day's attendance.

<Figure src="/projects/techdetect/ME.png" alt="Grid of grayscale photos of my face used for training" caption="the photos it took of me while enrolling" />

It also used Google's MediaPipe to track hands, so it could tell when someone raised one, and whose hand it was.

<Figure src="/projects/techdetect/Attendance.png" alt="TechDetect's attendance web page" caption="the attendance page" />

## What came of it

TechDetect won the 2022 Congressional App Challenge for Georgia's 5th district, and I got to meet Rep. Nikema Williams. Looking back, the code is very high school. But it's the first thing I built that worked end to end and that other people cared about.
